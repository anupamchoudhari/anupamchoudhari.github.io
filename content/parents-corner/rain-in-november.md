Hi Kiddo,

On Saturday we went to our first rock concert together. I've been trying to work out whether we got there by luck or by destiny, so I've put together a briefing, and you can decide. You're the one binge-reading the [Cherub series](https://www.amazon.in/Cherub/dp/B074BZ2PMC). You know a dramatic plot when you see one.

## Mission briefing

**Intel 1.** It rained. In November. We did the only sensible thing and asked Alexa for the song. Alexa obliged, loudly, more than once. Then on Saturday the band played it too. The sky stayed dry that night (it had clearly used up its November quota already), but a field full of people sang it anyway. Felt a lot like the universe had sent us the trailer first.

**Intel 2.** You told *me* about the concert. Not the other way round. When did that happen? When did you get big enough to know what's playing in our city before your father does? And where did you even find a [Guns N' Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guns_N%27_Roses) connection, when your playlist is wall-to-wall [Katseye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katseye), Shakira and Taylor Swift?

**Intel 3.** I was halfway through the audiobook of [*The Storyteller*](https://www.audible.in/pd/The-Storyteller-Expanded-Audiobook/B0C15K8XS6), [Dave Grohl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Grohl)'s memoir. (Drummer in Nirvana, then the guy who started [Foo Fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foo_Fighters). You'll hear his songs soon. I'll make sure of it.) A lot of the book is about raising his three girls. A lot of it is about his mom, a schoolteacher, who used to take him and his sister to Sunday afternoon jazz sessions at a tiny club in Washington, DC, to hear local musicians play. So there I was, listening to a rock star talk about his mom taking him to see live music, while my kid was telling me about a gig in town. Very subtle, universe.

**Intel 4.** Your mom, on one of her umpteen WhatsApp groups, found a stranger selling his concert tickets because his flight got cancelled. Two tickets. Exactly two. (Sorry, Mom.) What say, universe? And sorry you guys missed it, Sheeraz and Neel (strangers no more). Also, thank you.

**Mission status:** destiny, with some help from an airline.

## The show

The band came on late, and [Axl Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axl_Rose) told us why: Bangalore traffic. Welcome, Axl. You're one of us now.

The venue, (very) NICE Grounds, was way out on Tumakuru Road, so most of the crowd had their own traffic story before the first chord. We got lucky and took the metro, where our only traffic was a bogie full of fellow rockers in black tees with the five skulls on a cross from the [*Appetite for Destruction*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appetite_for_Destruction) cover. At CHERUB, a black T-shirt is the highest honour. At a Guns N' Roses concert, it's the dress code.

And then Axl started running, and doing side-to-side shuffles, for the next two and a half hours. I looked it up afterwards because I didn't believe it: he is 64. The man sprinted from one end of that stage to the other, jumped, spun, swung the mic stand around, and came back for more. Then, out of nowhere, he was sitting at a piano for the opening of November Rain, as if he'd been there all along.

Then there's [Slash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash_(musician)). The hat, the hair, barely a word into the microphone, and a different guitar every few songs. How did he keep switching? We wondered. Then the hat gave it away. He's obviously from Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry.

What got me wasn't the noise. It was how long they've been doing it. The band started in 1985, which makes it about 40 years of touring, getting on planes, showing up in cities they can't pronounce and playing songs they've played thousands of times as if the crowd in front of them was the first one. Some nights the voice doesn't do everything it used to. They show up anyway and give it everything they've got.

And the crowd. Tens of thousands of people who came, mostly, to see two men. Most of them, me included, knew about five songs. (Mine: Welcome to the Jungle, Sweet Child O' Mine, November Rain, Knockin' on Heaven's Door, Paradise City. No shame.) Plenty of them had spent two hours one way just getting there. Nobody cared. When one of the five came on, the whole field turned into one very loud, slightly off-key choir. And somewhere in that deafening hum of the bass, you'd lean in and whisper in my ear, excited and a little apologetic: "Which song is this?" I loved every one of those.

## My personal rock star

The best performances I've seen lately weren't on a stage next to Madavara metro station, though.

Slash had a rack of guitars and, apparently, a wand. You have one guitar, and you still got me. I want you to understand how strange that is from where I'm standing. I never learnt an instrument. I never even pictured myself trying. My hands know a QWERTY keyboard and my prized [Timemore C3S Pro](https://amzn.in/d/0dDxu1vr) coffee grinder, and that's about it.

Then one afternoon you played and sang at the same time. [*Dandelions*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W8a4sUabCUo). Your fingers doing one thing, your voice doing another, and both of them landing where they were supposed to. I can barely walk and talk at the same time without walking into a door. How did you get here? I've been in the next room the whole time, and I still don't know.

The other day you performed a monologue from *Alice in Wonderland*. Five minutes, on your own, your expressive voice modulating up and down, your mime saying a thousand words a minute, a whole other person coming out of you.

I would not remember a single line. I walk into the kitchen and forget why I'm there.

Just keep doing it. Do it for me, if you ever need an audience of one. The applause, the other parents filming on their phones, the judges trying to score you on technicalities: they come and go. I'll be in the front row whether there's a front row or not.

That's it. That's the letter.

Love,<br>
Dad

P.S. The next concert is your pick too. Apparently you know this city better than I do now.
